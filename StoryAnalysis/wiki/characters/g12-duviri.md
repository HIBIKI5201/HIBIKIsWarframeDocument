#### Lore

##### Lost Islands of Duviri

According to Acrithis' audio logs, Duviri is but a shell of its former self. The kingdom used to have many more islands, so many that Acrithis had been able to fly until the Archarbor was out of sight, and then on again, unable to number the lands over which she flew. All islands with the banner of Dominus Thrax. However, most were consumed by the Void, and some were removed by Thrax himself, whether out of fear of mysterious phenomena or to punish insolent and defiant citizens.

##### The Duviri Paradox

Ruling Duviri with an iron fist, Dominus Thrax views others within Duviri as his toys and actors in a violent fairy tale, using his time-reversal ability to undo deaths in the story he is trying to tell. He treats the Drifter as his personal plaything and a character to be executed in his dramas, and is insulted by their attempts to leave Duviri.

One day, the Drifter receives assistance from outside the Void and with the help of Teshin is able to fight back in Duviri. As the Drifter discovers parts of a toy figurine whose paradoxical nature may help them escape, Dominus Thrax increases his search for them and eventually has Teshin captured and killed.

An enraged Drifter possesses an Orowyrm and assaults Dominus Thrax's throne, reclaiming all the pieces of the toy figurine. The toy takes the form of Thrax himself and the Drifter realizes that Duviri and its inhabitants were created from a storybook through Conceptual Embodiment from their own anxieties after the Zariman Ten Zero's Void jump accident. As Thrax quickly snatches the figurine, the Drifter takes a seat on the throne and uses its power to reverse time once more, resurrecting Teshin but returning the throne to Thrax, which the Drifter deems to be a fair trade. Now aware of the truth behind Duviri, Thrax no longer has any hold over the Drifter and they are able to leave Duviri if they so wish.

##### Isleweaver (Operation: Eight Claw)

Following the events of The Hex, Dominus Thrax has been overthrown by Major Neci Rusalka who has been possessed by The Indifference. Thrax is forced to turn to the Tenno to defeat the usurper.

He can be seen on concept art with another person; that other person lifting up what looks like a cape or syandana, assuming a very low posture, indicating some sort of hierarchy between them, almost like master and slave. This may also indicate that more people might be present in Duviri.

  - This was confirmed during the TennoLive stream 2022, shown with a cast of many other characters, such as his servants Bombastine and Luscinia, as well as the civilians seen throughout the world. However, the servant in the concept art was not present in the demo nor the final release, presumably scrapped.

His name was first revealed in the Art Panel during TennoCon 2022. Curiously, he shares a name with the Thrax Centurion and Thrax Legatus, implying a connection between them.

  - Thrax later reveals during Operation: Eight Claw that he needs the Centurions in order to manifest beyond the Dormizone, suggesting that the manifestations directly serve him.
- Changes to Duviri are ascribed by Digital Extremes to be due to his emotional state at any point in time; this also affects the colors visible in the normally monochromatic Duviri, with red hues indicating he is angered and blue ones indicating he is upset and sad. While the monochromatic nature was mostly lost in the full release, his moods still affect the skybox and general hues during each respective Spiral cycle (red for Anger, yellow for Joy, green for Envy, blue for Fear, and gray/white for Sorrow).
- He is referred to as the "Child King", as well as the “Mad King” in a Duviri press release for Devstream 168 on the Digital Extremes website.

- Some Duviri citizens refer to the Drifter as their King, foreshadowing them to be the true ruler of Duviri.
- Thrax seems to be aware that he is a fictional character in his own, often making remarks about stories and even at one point indirectly mentions the Tales of Duviri book during Operation: Eight Claw.

#### Lore

A flamboyant actor and playwright, Bombastine suffers from extreme pride and jealousy, and is unable to love himself. He harbors deep envy for his love interest, Luscinia, whom Dominus Thrax favors more. He claims he never loved her, but when he thinks no one is listening he secretly cries over her and confesses his love for her.

##### Tales of Duviri - The Covetous Courtier

> “Bombastine was envious. Of what? Anything. Wherever his gaze fell, it seemed other people had it better.”

> —Euleria Entrati

Bombastine was envious of anything and everyone. It seemed other people had it better wherever he looked. Although he was Duviri's "preening peacock", and the subject of envy by many, he never felt that it was enough and did not care for it. Many loved him, but others were loved more and that was poison to him. Even when he persuaded others with sweet words, he only ever thought how his performance could benefit him. Each new conquest was a chance to stop, but he kept indulging in a new fixation like a child instead until he eventually set his heart on the throne. Bombastine did not realize that many people suffered from self-doubt, thinking it was his alone. Not loving himself eventually made him hurl himself into disaster, and he was consumed by his Envy.

#### Lore

Luscinia is Dominus Thrax's favorite singer; the envious Bombastine once tried to break her but instead made her Dominus Thrax's chosen; she now serves as his primary entertainer. There was a time when Duviri's festivals were held on Lake Verula as its main spot. Within the lake, a school of rainbow eels would be attracted by the music and would follow Luscinia across, floundering on the shore when she disembarked, as if they yearned to remain with her.

##### Tales of Duviri - The Sorrowful Soprano

Luscinia indulged in sorrow in excess. It is a poison that Luscinia drank like wine. She wondered if inflicting suffering upon those who deserved it would end her misery, but she eventually realized that though it comforted her at first, it was a cold comfort and her sorrow grew. Sorrow made Luscinia compassionate for she could not give pain without feeling it and she wept for those she was hurting. She tried to dismiss this thought as a weakness but her heart disagreed. Luscinia eventually decided to walk down the dark path she had set her feet upon to the very end. She already knew the outcome, like a prophet's gift, but she could not turn aside, even if she prepared to risk everything on a reckless gamble.

#### Lore & History

##### General

Sythel is a deeply anxious and fearful woman, and can often be found in her boarded-up house with her ear to the wall. Due to her severe paranoia, her home has been left in disarray, with numerous cobwebs and candle-like lanterns littering its interior.

Sythel has an intense fear of the Void, but since Duviri resides in the Void, she is left in an indefinite state of fear and paranoia. Her severe paranoia extends to everyone in Duviri, even Dominus Thrax himself; she believes that everyone and everything, even the Tamms, are plotting against her.

##### Lost Islands of Duviri

Sythel mentions how the first scholar to peer into the Void became afraid of it, and that his fear took shape and became real. It is believed that this scholar was Albrecht Entrati.

Sythel also talks about the Hollow Children, a group of Void manifestations that took on the appearance of black-eyed children in Duviri. Their appearance led to the loss of Academe, so it can be presumed that Sythel was around before the island was lost.

When losing control of her emotions, Sythel will also comment on the Drifter having similar eyes to the children.

> “Afraid to go outside and afraid to be shut in, Sythel lived a timorous life. Usually, she was too scared to act… but not always.”

She had a thought that if she destroyed what she feared then she wouldn't have to be afraid anymore: however, she was frightened by what she had done and began to worry about the consequences. The more brave she tried to be, the more she feared being discovered and soon her fear began to soak all throughout Duviri itself causing the citizens to shut their doors, barre their windows and talk only in whispers. Her resolve began to crack as her plans led to disaster after disaster. For an instant, Sythel would no longer be afraid when she shed her human form, for she had become Fear itself.

#### General

Lodun's title of 'The Prince of Fire' is, in fact, a sarcastic insult, as he possesses no real authority beyond his executions. He believes himself to be the rightful King of Duviri, but due to the complications of his ancestry, was supplanted by Dominus Thrax. As consolation, Lodun instead received the position as Thrax's personal executioner.

Some of Duviri's citizens believe Lodun should be the one on the throne, however, they are quickly hunted down by the Dax and sentenced to death.

Additionally, Dominus Thrax seems to dislike Lodun, and based on his claims, will often attempt to sabotage Lodun from proving his lineage by confiscating and hiding his royal regalia, hiding or burning the succession scrolls, and sending squads of Dax after him. The feelings are mutual, as Lodun is also quite frustrated over Thrax's neglect of his duties over Duviri. Thus, it is possible that Lodun acts out of genuine care to provide order to the kingdom, but falls short due to his uncontrollable temper getting in the way.

When not the featured courtier, Lodun can be found in front of his home, and appears to be burning a pile of books.

#### Lore & History

##### Tales of Duviri - The Prince of Fire

Main article: Tales of Duviri

> “Hear now of the one they called Lodun – the high and the mighty prince of…nothing. For within him burned a fire and a fury he could not control.”

> —Euleria Entrati

Lodun, along with the rest of Duviri, was originally created as a storybook character in the "Tales of Duviri" book, written by Euleria Entrati. He was written as an example of what happens when one fails to master themselves and their anger, and as a result, is consumed by it.

Lodun of Duviri, was nicknamed 'the Prince of Fire', which was a mocking title, for he ruled over nothing, least of all himself. The smallest of things could spark his anger. He drove people away with his anger and lashed out at friends and foes alike, and it became a weight in his chest until it made him an enemy to even himself. He eventually realized that the source of his anger was his own shortcomings, and unable to contain it, he was consumed by it.

##### Lost Islands of Duviri

When the Hollow Children appeared on the island of Academe, Thrax ordered Lodun to destroy the island, but when he arrived there, he found it had broken free from the rest of the islands on its own. Thrax didn't give Lodun the order to pursue the island.

#### Lore

Mathila seemingly owns or runs a farm with her two children, Barris and Koral. She believes that happiness is the greatest thing in the world, and wishes to spread it among people whether they want it or not. Mathila displays severe signs of toxic positivity, and will even abandon rationality to prove her ideals of Happiness.

##### Lost Islands of Duviri

After Teshin was cast into the Void portal during the events of the New War, he arrived in Duviri. Mathila was the one who found Teshin in a lake on the island known as Hermit's Landing, and was the one who pulled him ashore and nursed him back to health.

Mathila also had a husband, Garmi, but appears to have no recollection of him. Garmi was once the keeper of the Seriglass Lighthouse on Watcher's Island. The lighthouse had no apparent purpose, yet Garmi kept its light burning, stating that he was keeping 'it' at bay. One day, a very disastrous Void Storm struck Duviri, causing great lengths of chain to fall from the sky (presumably the Chains of Harrow, destroyed at the end of their namesake quest). These chains rained down destroying everything and everyone they hit, even Garmi's lighthouse.

Following the incident, Mathila rarely talks about her husband. Whenever Acrithis attempts to bring him up, Mathila merely freezes, unable to speak. Koral mentions that her mother says that Garmi was taken by the sea, implying Mathila does know of his fate and refuses speak of it in public in order to remain happy, and that is why Mathila is always wary of them being near water.

##### Tales of Duviri - The Harbinger of Joy

> “To the Happy mind, all things seem simple. Problems could be solved simply by making them go away.”

> —Euleria Entrati

Mathila believed that she knew what would make people happy better than they did. She believed that problems can be solved simply by making them go away.

However, Mathila was a like a child who hid their face playfully, attempting to hide from the truth as nothing but Joy mattered to her.

She did not understand why others were complicated, nor did she understand that making unhappiness a crime was not a way to create happiness. She embraced optimism with a fanatic-like zeal despite her plans failing.

She hid her doubts, and became determined to succeed: however, there was no logic or reason to her plans. No sane person would look at Mathila's work and feel happy, so she abandoned reason and went from being happy to going mad.

##### Isleweaver

The Indifference, possessing the body of Major Neci Rusalka, uses Mathila's refusal or ignorance of Garmi's fate to invade Duviri during her spiral, deposing Dominus Thrax and slowly merging it with the greater Void. Despite her unwitting role in the invasion, Mathila periodically aids Thrax and the Drifter in reclaiming Duviri, directing them towards contingencies left behind by Albrecht Entrati.

#### General

Appearance-wise, Rablits bear a close resemblance to the ancient rabbits of pre-Orokin Earth. Unlike rabbits, who have four legs, Rablits walk exclusively on their hind legs, while their frontal limbs have developed into full-blown arms. Rablits also have a pair of metallic antlers.

Unlike most Rablits, who have brown bodies, exposed eyeless skulls and ribs, Teshin's Rablit has a pale blue body, and flesh covers all of her bones. This also leads to her uniquely having a fleshy face, with eyes and a nose. Her left horn is also broken and a strip of cloth hangs from the right horn.

Despite their small stature, Rablits can become ferocious if provoked and are capable of killing Dax. They are often preyed upon by Kexats.

#### Trivia

- When engaging in Komi during The Duviri Experience, Teshin's Rablit spectates the board game match on the table, or presumably is the Drifter's opponent.
- Once all six stages of a Mood Spiral is complete, Teshin's Rablit appears at the nearest Materlith carrying his Orvius. When the Drifter equips the Orvius to trigger the Orowyrm Grand Boss fight, the Rablit then burrows underground to retreat back to Teshin's Cave.
- Teshin's Rablit's names are all feminine in origin. Sól is the Norse goddess of the Sun, Terra is the Roman personification of the Earth, and Lua is the Portuguese and Galician word from the Latin Luna, the Roman personification of the Moon.
- The Rablits' design seems to be similar to the jackalope, a mythical animal described as a hare with antlers.
- During the final dialogue with Teshin in the end of The Duviri Paradox quest, the scenery changes depending on the choice for the name of the Rablit.
